Idea for finding rockets when the azimuth is gone

Most of the time when we lose one it isn't because nobody saw it. Several people
watched it come down and can point right at it. But a bearing only exists from
where you were standing. Walk around a treeline or down into a draw and back up,
and the line is gone.

I've been playing with an app called Theodolite (iOS) that overlays true bearing
and lat/lon on the camera view and logs both with one button press. The idea:
two people standing 100-150 m apart each put the crosshair on the landing and
press LOG. Cross the two bearings, get a coordinate, hand-jam it into a handheld
and walk to it. No line of sight needed once you have the number.

I tried it this week on campus using a building spire as a stand-in target - two
positions about 150 m apart, target a few hundred meters out, roughly the
geometry we'd have at the field. Crossed the two bearings and the fix landed
essentially on the spire. Small sample, but encouraging enough that I wanted to
ask about trying it for real.

The thing that seems to matter most is spreading the two spotters out so they
straddle the landing area, rather than both standing on the flight line. If the
two sight lines come in at a shallow angle the intersection gets sloppy.

What I'd like to try: a few flights with a rocket carrying a tracker we already
trust, so we can compare the two-spotter fix against the tracker's readout and
against a GPS reading taken standing at the rocket. If those agree we've got
something. If they don't, I'd rather find out now.

That would take a flier putting up a tracked rocket who doesn't mind us running
the spotters on it, and two people willing to stand at fixed spots and not chase.

To be up front: this isn't a replacement for a tracker. If you've got one, use
it. I'm thinking about the untracked flights, and particularly the ones where the
rocket drops behind trees or over a fence line and whoever saw it has to cover a
quarter mile of terrain to get there.

I've got a rough calculator that takes the two log files and spits out a
coordinate plus a KML you can open in Google Earth. Glad to share it, or to walk
anyone through the app setup, if there's interest.
